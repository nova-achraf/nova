How to compress an image

  1. Drop your images or tap the box to select them.
  2. Pick a quality level — 70–80% is visually identical to the original for most photos.
  3. Optionally limit the width (great for websites and email).
  4. Download each file, or press “Download all”.

Why compress images?

A 12-megapixel phone photo is often 4–6 MB. That slows down websites, fills up email inboxes and gets rejected by upload forms with size limits. Compressing to 300–500 KB usually keeps the image indistinguishable to the eye while cutting the size by 80–90%.

Is my data safe?

Yes. This tool uses the HTML5 Canvas API inside your browser. Your images are read locally and are never sent to a server — there is no upload step at all. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it will still work.

Which format should I choose?

  • JPG — best for photos, smallest files.
  • WebP — 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same quality; supported by all modern browsers.
  • PNG — use only for logos, screenshots and images that need transparency.

Common questions

Will compressing ruin my photo? At 70–85% quality, no. Below 50% you may notice artefacts in gradients and fine detail.

What is the maximum file size? There is no server, so it depends on your device memory. Photos up to ~40 MB normally work fine on a phone.

Can I compress HEIC photos from iPhone? Most browsers cannot decode HEIC directly. Export them as JPG from the Photos app first.
